Furniture: move, place, rotate, take apart, repair, and 3D placement

Vanilla moves furniture with a world cursor in five modes, switched from a pop-up on the left-edge furniture button or a controller wheel, and explains the tile under the cursor in a floating box that trails the mouse. ZeD hid that left-edge button, so today the mouse has no way in. This mockup gives furniture a Furniture tab in the World window: the five modes, a full reading of the tile you point at, the objects on that tile, and the furniture you carry. A small chip at the cursor keeps the one line you need where you are looking. Vanilla's "Extended 3D Placement" window becomes a placement card in the card drawer. Switch the scenes above.

Decisions for you

  1. RECOMMENDA Furniture tab in the World window. It sits after Building and brings back the mouse's way into furniture mode, which went away with the left-edge buttons. Opening the tab starts the furniture cursor; leaving the tab, or B, ends it. Every vanilla way in still works (the controller wheel's Move furniture, an item's Install furniture, dragging furniture out of the inventory) and lands on the tab.
  2. RECOMMENDThe five modes are a strip on the tab. PICK UP, PLACE, ROTATE, DISASSEMBLE, REPAIR, in vanilla's words, replace the button pop-up, the controller wheel and the mode key's cycling. The cycle key keeps working and lights the strip.
  3. RECOMMENDThe tab reads the tile you point at. A preview of the object, its name, a status line, then vanilla's facts as a checklist: encumbrance against what you can carry, the skill and its level, each tool with a dot for whether you have it, the chance to break, and whether it rotates. Vanilla's blockers ("Has items in container.", "Too heavy for inventory.", "Is being sat on."...) become NEEDS lines.
  4. RECOMMENDLists instead of the cycle key. Vanilla cycles through the objects on a tile, or the furniture you carry, with one key. The tab lists both: ON THIS TILE (pick which one to lift or take apart) and YOUR FURNITURE (pick which one goes on the cursor). FACING buttons (N, E, S, W) set the direction when placing or rotating, alongside vanilla's hold-and-drag.
  5. RECOMMENDDISASSEMBLE shows what it may give. Vanilla only shows the chance to break. The tab adds MAY YIELD from vanilla's own scrap table (a wooden chair: up to 6 planks, 4 nails, 4 screws, each with its roll chance). REPAIR shows the damage word and the parts it takes, with what you have.
  6. RECOMMENDA chip at the cursor. The tab is across the screen from where you look, so a small chip beside the target tile carries the name and one line: the mode when it can go ahead, or the first blocker in red. It replaces vanilla's floating box (and its position-cycling key).
  7. RECOMMENDExtended 3D Placement is a placement card. It opens in the card drawer from the right-click option. MOVEMENT and ROTATION each have three sliders (X, Y, Z) with nudge buttons (0.01 of a tile, 15 degrees), REVERT and DONE. Vanilla's world gizmo stays: it shows the move arrows while you work in MOVEMENT and the turn rings in ROTATION. The card stays within vanilla's 3 tiles.
  8. On a controller. Pick the mode, the object or the facing on the tab; B hands the stick back to the world cursor, and the tab keeps reading the tile you aim at.
