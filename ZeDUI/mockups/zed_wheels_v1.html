Wheels

Vanilla's radial menus are one wheel per player (and a second one for the controller's Back button), filled by thirteen callers: the vehicle (V or D-pad Up, in and out of a car), animals, emotes (hold Q or D-pad Down, with categories), the firearm (hold R or RB), light sources (hold F), the map (hold M), the controller's Back, D-pad Right, left-stick and furniture wheels, and the hotbar slots. The engine draws them all the same way: a dark 400 px disc over the middle of the screen, colour pictures round it, and the pointed slice's words white in the middle. Here every one of them becomes the ZeD context strip's cells bent into a ring, with the strip's bubble above naming the pointed slice. Slices sit exactly where vanilla puts them, so every flick of the stick and sweep of the mouse lands where it always has. Backdrops are real captures of the game at 1080p; switch Look to Vanilla for the same moment today.

Decisions for you

  1. RECOMMENDKeep it a wheel, on vanilla's geometry. Slice 1 centred at 12 o'clock, the rest clockwise, each the same share of the circle; an empty place stays empty (the firearm wheel's three positions never move). Picking is vanilla's: the pointer's direction or the right stick picks; a click, or letting go of the key or button that opened it, takes; B, X and Escape close. YOUR CALL the context strip itself (a row, picked with left / right): one menu for everything, but every direction players have learned is gone.
  2. RECOMMENDThe strip's cells on a ring. The same paper cells with ink icons, the pointed cell in the accent, the System 7 shadow; a thin ring line and a small hub show the wheel's centre. No dark disc: vanilla's covers your character and everything round them; this takes about a third of the room.
  3. RECOMMENDThe name in the strip's bubble, above. Vanilla prints the pointed slice's words white on dark in the middle of the disc. Here the bubble above the ring names it in bold, with vanilla's further lines under it (the firearm's counts, the item a light slice is about, the verb). Nothing pointed, nothing shown; inside an emote category the bubble names the category.
  4. RECOMMENDSlices that can't be done read as off. Vanilla adds some slices with no action ("No key. You need Electrical level 1 and Mechanics level 2 to hotwire.", "Not tired enough to sleep.", "Stop the car first.", "Need a hose.") that look like any other. They are stippled like the strip's off cells; pointed, the bubble gives vanilla's reason with a red dot.
  5. RECOMMENDPictures: ZeD's icons where the meaning is plain (the vehicle, maps, furniture, animals, most of the Back wheel), vanilla's own pictures where they say more than an icon can (the gestures, the firearm's steps, zoom and game speed), and items as their own pictures. YOUR CALL vanilla's pictures everywhere (switch Pictures).
  6. YOUR CALLCell size. The strip's own cells (22) keep the two menus identical; larger cells (33, recommended) let the gestures and the firearm steps read. Either way the wheel is far smaller than vanilla's.
  7. Emote categories. A category's cell has the strip's corner triangle; choosing it swaps the ring in place, as vanilla does. Vanilla's Back stays where vanilla puts it (the last slice, just left of 12 o'clock), drawn as the strip's accent back cell.
  8. No fade, no zoom-in. The wheel is there at once, and gone at once (1993). Vanilla's sounds stay (the vehicle wheel's open and close).
  9. Already ZeD: the hotbar slot wheel (hold 1 to 8) and the controller's D-pad Left wheel are the belt's lists. Vanilla's three wheel options stay as they are (show / hide with each key press; the reload wheel at once; the left-stick wheel on or off).
