Vehicle Mechanics

The vanilla mechanics window becomes a Vehicle tab in the World window, contextual like Radio: it appears when a vehicle is within reach and holds everything the vanilla screen does. It follows the Health tab's shape (figure, summary, part list) and every part opens as an item card with its verbs in the header. What each action needs is shown on the card before you press it, instead of in hover tooltips. Click any part in the figure or the list. Switch the states, the input mode and the theme above the window.

Decisions for you

  1. RECOMMENDWhere it lives. A Vehicle tab in the World window's contextual group, after Animals. It shows when a vehicle is within reach and carries a count badge when there are several, browsed with the arrows in the tab header, the same way Radio browses radios. The vanilla Mechanics option (right-click, the hood, the V menu) lands here too.
  2. RECOMMENDKeep the inspection step. Vanilla makes you walk to the car and look it over (faster with more Mechanics skill) before you see part conditions. The tab keeps that: before an inspection it shows the car's name and type and an INSPECT button in the footer (see "Not inspected"). After inspecting, the full view stays open until you walk away, as vanilla closes past six tiles.
  3. RECOMMENDThe car figure. It sits where the body figure sits on the Health tab and uses the same scanline look and grid box, so the two read as one family. Vanilla already has top-down part diagrams for every body shape (sedan, station wagon, small car, sports car, SUV, off-road, pickup, race car, van, three trailers). A small script can turn those into ZeD scanline masks automatically, the way the plate pipeline turned the dash mockups into art. The alternative is to draw the figures by hand, like the body figure; that is more work and more charm. Your call on art direction.
  4. RECOMMENDPart cards are item cards. A part opens as the 480-wide item card: back arrow, name, verbs in the header, the progress band while you work. The NEEDS section lists what the highlighted verb needs (tools, skill, recipe, other parts that must be on or off, an empty tank, the key) with a dot for each, and the success and failure chance when there is a risk. Hovering or focusing another verb switches the list. Nothing hides in tooltips.
  5. RECOMMENDInstalling without submenus. A missing part's card lists every compatible part you can reach, from your bags and from nearby containers such as the trunk, with condition and where it is. Pick one, press INSTALL. Types you do not have stay listed, dimmed, so you know what to look for.
  6. RECOMMENDConfirm only what destroys. Taking engine parts (it lowers the engine's condition) and smashing a window swap the footer to a YES / NO confirmation, the Animals tab pattern. Everything else runs on the first press, as in vanilla.
  7. RECOMMENDGamepad. The part list is the focus path; the figure follows the focused part instead of being a second set of stops. A opens a part, X opens the context menu with the same verbs, B goes back from a card to the list, Y closes the windows as usual. On a card the D-pad moves across the header verbs and down into the pick list. Switch Input to Gamepad to see the focus cue and the footer prompts. The figure scrolls away with the list, because a tab scrolls as one piece; pinning it would need an exception to that rule.
  8. The hood. Opening Mechanics through the hood opens it; vanilla closes it when the window closes. Here the footer offers CLOSE HOOD while it is open, and the hood closes by itself when you walk away.
  9. Not in this tab. Seat switching and the vehicle radial stay vanilla for now, animals in trailers are their own ledger row, and the debug cheat options stay in the debug-only context menu.
