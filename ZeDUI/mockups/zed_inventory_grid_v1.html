Inventory grid view

An optional grid view for any inventory column, chosen per column with a list | grid toggle in the column's heading; the list stays the default. Shown in the World window's Loot tab with three containers open as columns, the counter in grid view: each item a square cell with its icon, the stack count, a thin condition bar and its weight in the corner. Selecting a cell opens the same item card the list opens. The category headings stay; sorting stays the list's job. The item pictures here are stand-ins for the game's own icons.

Decisions for you

  1. RECOMMENDA list | grid toggle in every column's heading, two small icon buttons beside the weight (each names itself in a hover hint: "List view", "Grid view"). The list is the default everywhere. The choice is remembered per container (the fridge you set to grid opens as a grid next time), in both windows' columns.
  2. YOUR CALLRemember the choice per kind of container (every fridge a grid) instead of per container. Fewer clicks; less control.
  3. RECOMMENDThe cell, 52 x 52 (60 at Large text): the item's icon at 32, the stack count bottom right ("x4") on a dark backing so it reads over any icon, the weight of the cell (the whole stack) top right in data type, a 2 px condition bar along the bottom only for items that have condition (green, then warning under 60%, danger under 25%, as the list's bar). A worn or equipped item keeps the list's tag as a small corner badge; a key on the belt keeps its BELT n tag the same way.
  4. RECOMMENDStates: pointer over a cell is the 0.22 accent fill and a hover hint with the item's name (a cell has no words, so the hint earns its place); the selected cell is a deeper accent fill with the icon darkened, as System 7 selects an icon; controller focus is the 0.55 fill. No outlines for focus.
  5. RECOMMENDSelecting a cell opens the item card in the drawer, exactly as selecting a row does today: the same header verbs, NEEDS lines, facts and condition. Selecting another cell swaps the card; selecting the same one closes it. A stack opens the stack's card as the list's stack row does.
  6. RECOMMENDDrag and drop as the list: drag a cell to another column (the column lights with the accent frame and its heading shows the weight after the move), onto a bag's cell (into the bag), onto the belt or a hand. A drop that cannot happen (too heavy, wrong place) shows the column dimmed and the reason in the heading, as the list does.
  7. RECOMMENDWhat stays list-only: sorting. The column heads (NAME, CAT, COND, WT) and their sort live in the list; the grid shows the items in the list's current order, named in the thin bar under the heading ("ORDER: NAME, AS THE LIST"). Category headings, folding a category and stacks stay as they are. No new sorting, no "sort by value", nothing vanilla does not do.
  8. RECOMMENDController in the grid: the D-pad moves cell to cell in four directions. Up / down cross category headings to the cell nearest in the row above or below and never leave the column (as today, up / down stay in the panel); up from the first row lands on the heading's toggle. Left / right walk the row; at a row's end they cross to the next column or strip (left / right cross panels, as today), never wrapping to the next row. A toggles the card, X is the item's menu, hold X picks the item up and the D-pad carries it to a cell, a column or the belt; releasing X drops it. The column scrolls to keep the focused cell in view.
  9. ALTERNATIVECells with the name under the icon (52 x 70). Easier to read without hovering, but a third fewer cells fit, which is most of the reason for the grid.
