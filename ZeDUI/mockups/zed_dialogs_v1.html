Prompts

Vanilla asks its questions in small grey pop-ups: a black box, white words in the middle, grey Yes and No under them. They come from a handful of vanilla pieces (a question or note, a long rich-text note, a text box, and the alarm and timer setters) opened from about sixty places in play: sleeping, killing an animal, renaming a bag, setting an alarm, the help behind a window's ?, every request from another player, and from mods. This mockup gives all of them one ZeD prompt with vanilla's own words and answers. The backdrop is a real capture of the game at 1080p; switch Look to Vanilla to see the same moment today.

Decisions for you

  1. RECOMMENDOne prompt for all of them. ZeD steps in where vanilla builds each pop-up and opens a prompt made from ZeD's own window, buttons and fields instead. The answer goes back to the game exactly as vanilla's would (the same Yes, No, OK and typed text), so nothing about what happens next changes. Mods that use vanilla's pop-ups get the ZeD prompt without doing anything.
  2. RECOMMENDThe shape. A small ZeD window: the striped title bar with the close box and no title (System 7's movable dialog; the words are vanilla's question, so a title would only repeat it), the words in the body, the answers in the footer on the right with the main answer last. The close box gives the same answer as No or Cancel (OK on a note; on the alarm it leaves the alarm as it was).
  3. RECOMMENDSystem 7's marks. A question mark beside a question, an "i" beside a note, and the stop sign beside a question that costs something: kill an animal, remove a zone, remove someone from a safehouse or faction, delete items, quit after death. On those, YES is the red pulsing button the menus use for Delete. The list of costly questions is short and ours; any other question, a mod's included, gets the question mark.
  4. RECOMMENDWhere it opens. Where vanilla opens it: on the pointer for the mouse (the click that asked), in the middle of your part of the screen with a controller, and in the middle for the alarm, the timer and the help. Always kept wholly on screen (vanilla's hangs off the edge when you click near one). YOUR CALL always the middle of the screen (switch Where).
  5. RECOMMENDThe game keeps going. No dimming and no pause, as in vanilla: a prompt never hides what is coming at you. It can be dragged by its title bar.
  6. Typing. The menus' form: the label in capitals over the field, Enter answers OK, Escape cancels. When OK cannot be pressed, the reason sits under the field with a red dot (vanilla greys the button and hides the reason in a tooltip): "Can't be empty.", "Character limit: 28", or the caller's own reason.
  7. Numbers. The Options page's stepper (− value +). The alarm: hour (0 to 23, wrapping) and minutes (in tens, vanilla's steps) with the Alarm tick. The timer keeps vanilla's −10 −5 −1 +1 +5 +10 around the value. The mouse wheel over a value steps it; typing still works.
  8. Long notes. The help behind a window's ? and the like: vanilla's rich text set in ZeD type (its title as a heading, its paragraphs), the body scrolling as one, OK in the footer.
  9. RECOMMENDOn a controller. The focus starts on the main answer (or the field, as vanilla's does); the D-pad moves between answers, rows and the field, left and right step a stepper; A presses; B is No or Cancel, as in vanilla. The window's slim strip under the footer names the buttons, like the World window's. A on a field opens the keyboard.
  10. YOUR CALLThe keyboard. A ZeD keyboard (scene Keyboard): vanilla's keys and its button map (X delete, Y space, LT shift, L3 caps, RT accept, B cancel) on ZeD keys, docked under the prompt; the menus would use it too. Or keep vanilla's keyboard, which the menus use today.
  11. Not here. The map's note and symbol pop-ups (ZeD's map has its own notes and vanilla's are not reached), the menus' questions (already ZeD), and vanilla's sleep-hours pop-up (the game never opens it).
