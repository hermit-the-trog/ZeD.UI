Car Radio

The vehicle wheel's last vanilla window: Device Options, the stack of panels vanilla opens for a car's radio (power, signal, volume, the CD deck, the preset list, the microphone on a two-way set). Here it is the radio itself, brought up close above the dash the way the siren controller is, so the road stays in view. The face is the radio that is actually fitted: vanilla's own items are a ValuTech Radio, a Premium Technologies Radio (it takes CDs), a Makeshift Radio, and in police cars and radio vans a Premium Technologies Ham Radio, a US ARMY COMM. set or a makeshift one. Backdrops are real 1080p captures (the pickup fitted with the Premium Technologies radio; the sheriff car, whose dash slot holds a ham radio); Look: Vanilla shows vanilla's window, captured live where it opens.

Decisions for you

  1. RECOMMENDThe radio up close, as a prop above the dash. The same place and scale as the siren controller (its right edge on the dash console's right edge), so you can drive with it open. One up-close prop at a time: opening the radio puts the siren controller away, and back. The wheel's slice opens and closes it; Escape, B or leaving the car put it away.
  2. RECOMMENDIts face is the radio that is fitted. Each of vanilla's six radios gets its own face: the Premium Technologies CD receiver (cyan display, CD slot), the ValuTech budget radio (amber digits, presets on masking tape), the makeshift radio (the green board already on the dash), and three two-way sets for police cars and radio vans (the Premium Technologies rig shown here, an olive US ARMY COMM. set, a makeshift one). This mockup shows three; the other three follow the same pattern.
  3. YOUR CALLThe dash's own head unit could follow the fitted radio too. Today each car's console draws its own make (ValuTech in the trucks, Premium in the fleet cars), whatever radio is fitted. Swapping the console's head unit for the fitted radio's face (small) would make the dash and the close-up match.
  4. RECOMMENDEvery vanilla control, as hardware. Push the VOL knob for power, turn it for volume; MUTE; turn TUNE; SEEK jumps to the next station; SCAN stops on each for a moment. Presets: press to listen, hold to store (printed under the buttons, as real ones were); the first six of vanilla's presets are the six buttons. The display gives the frequency, the signal and the station's name (vanilla's channel name). With no power (a flat battery) the display stays dark and nothing answers; the dash's battery shows why.
  5. RECOMMENDCDs. Click the empty slot: a short list of the CDs you carry; pick one and it goes in (vanilla's own action). EJECT puts it back in your inventory; PLAY / STOP plays it. The display shows the disc and the track.
  6. RECOMMENDTwo-way sets keep vanilla's extras. The wide band (10 to 500 MHz) on a big dial, every stored channel on CH up and down (a ham set holds more than six), MEM stores the frequency as a new channel, holding MEM names it or forgets it (the ZeD text prompt), MIC mutes the microphone (its red lamp says so), and the range is printed on the set.
  7. Controller: the D-pad walks the controls (left and right along a row, up and down between rows); on a knob, up and down turn it; A presses; on a preset, A listens and X stores; B puts the radio away. The strip under it says so.
